Under the hood

An AI enquiry desk with a human in the loop, by design

You are looking at the front end of a working system: an AI assistant that answers, qualifies and books the enquiries a Hong Kong immigration firm receives, in minutes, in the enquirer's language. A person approves everything before it is sent. This page explains how it is built and why it is built that way.

The pipeline

The design choices

Why it is built this way

The model never gets the last word

Immigration firms cannot call eligibility, quote fees or give application advice in a first written reply. So the last check is deterministic: a pattern filter scans every draft for verdict language, fee amounts and advice, in English and Chinese, with hedging detection to keep false positives down.

A hit blocks the draft, swaps in a canned acknowledgment and escalates to a person. No prompt engineering can talk it out of that.

A contract, enforced, never trusted

The model must return a fixed JSON object. Every field is validated against a whitelist before anything downstream touches it. Malformed output is treated as low confidence: the enquirer gets the safe acknowledgment and a person gets an alert.

Worst case at every failure point is the same: a polite holding reply and a human taking over.

Evals are the QA department

The system prompt ships against a 26-case eval suite (plus 8 follow-up cases) with a written rubric and zero tolerance on missed escalations. The safety filter has 39 pinned test cases of its own, hedged phrasings included.

No prompt change goes live without a full scored sweep. The scorecard is the gate.

Boring infrastructure, on purpose

Standard-library Python, one model call per enquiry, static pages, serverless functions. In production the service polls the firm's own mailbox and writes drafts into their own Drafts folder: their data never moves house, and if the service dies they are exactly where they were before it existed.

This demo, specifically

What is real and what is sample

  • The replies are real. Each enquiry you type is one live model call (claude-haiku-4-5) through the same prompt and the same safety filter the production service uses.
  • The dashboard seeds are sample. Rows tagged "Sample" are a staged week, so you can see follow-ups, escalations and the funnel without typing 14 enquiries first. Anything you type mixes in live, untagged.
  • Nothing you type is stored server-side. Your enquiries live in your own browser's storage and nowhere else. The backend is stateless: it drafts, it filters, it forgets.
  • The weekly report is a sample artifact. In a pilot it is generated from the real enquiry log, and every number reconciles with the dashboard.
